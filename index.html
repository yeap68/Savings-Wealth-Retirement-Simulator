Savings, Wealth & Retirement Simulator

Build savings, grow and diversify wealth, and plan retirement income while understanding the effect of inflation.
About this simulator - purpose and how to use it
Purpose. This simulator helps you explore how savings, investment returns, portfolio allocation, withdrawals, inflation and retirement income can affect your finances over time. It is a planning and scenario tool, not a prediction of future returns.
How to use it: Choose the planner that matches your question. You do not have to start with Savings. A new saver may test RM500 per month, an existing investor may go directly to Wealth with RM500,000, and a retiree may go directly to Retirement with RM1 million.
Enter your capital and cash flows, choose a single or diversified portfolio, review the projection, and open the guide notes whenever a term is unfamiliar. Complete Financial Plan and Master Timeline are overview tools; they do not require the three planners to be used in sequence.

Savings

If I save regularly, what could I accumulate?
1. How much will you save?
Guide: building your savings
Starting savings is money you already have. Monthly savings is new money you add. An annual increase changes the monthly amount once each year.
Example: RM500 per month with a 3% annual increase becomes RM515 per month in Year 2 and RM530.45 in Year 3.
2. Where will your savings grow?
Guide: single vs diversified
Single investment uses one investment for all savings. Diversified lets existing savings and new monthly savings use different allocations.
Example: RM100,000 already saved could be 50% Bank Deposit / 50% MGS, while new RM1,000 monthly savings could be 20% / 80%.
3. Withdrawals - optional
Use this only if you expect money to leave the savings plan during the projection.
Guide: how withdrawals affect compounding
A withdrawal is removed at the selected time. That money no longer earns future returns.
Example: if RM100,000 is invested and RM20,000 is withdrawn in Year 3, only the remaining balance continues compounding after the withdrawal.
With one investment, the source is automatic. With a diversified portfolio, choose which investment funds the withdrawal.

5. Purchasing power
Guide: what is purchasing power?
Purchasing power describes how much goods and services your money can buy. Inflation usually means the same ringgit amount buys less in the future.
Example: RM500,000 received 20 years from now is equivalent to about RM277,000 in today's money if inflation averages 3% a year. Your account still contains RM500,000; this calculation only translates its future buying power into today's ringgit.

Wealth & Investment

What could happen to the capital I already have?
1. Capital and new investment
Guide: existing capital and new money
Existing capital is money already invested. Additional capital now is added immediately. Regular new investment is contributed each month.
Example: RM500,000 existing capital plus RM50,000 added now starts at RM550,000. RM2,000 per month is then invested throughout the projection.
2. Portfolio
Guide: Current Money % vs New Money %
Current Money % tells the simulator where existing capital is invested. New Money % tells it where future monthly contributions go.
Example: RM500,000 may be 30% Deposit / 30% MGS / 40% ETF, while new RM2,000 monthly investment may be 10% / 20% / 70%. Each holding compounds using its own planning return.
3. Withdrawals - optional
Guide: withdrawing from investments
A withdrawal reduces the selected investment at the chosen time, so subsequent growth is calculated on the reduced balance. A withdrawal is different from an investment loss.
Example: withdrawing RM50,000 from an ETF in Year 5 means that RM50,000 no longer participates in the ETF's later returns.
For a diversified portfolio, you choose the funding investment. To use two holdings, add two withdrawals for the same date.

5. Purchasing power and real wealth creation
Guide: nominal wealth vs real wealth
Nominal wealth is the future ringgit balance. Real wealth translates future money into today's purchasing power. Real wealth created goes one step further: it compares real ending wealth with starting capital and the inflation-adjusted value of money you contributed or withdrew.
Why timing matters: RM2,000 contributed next month and RM2,000 contributed 10 years from now do not have the same value in today's ringgit. The simulator discounts each monthly cash flow separately before calculating real wealth creation.

Retirement & Capital Preservation

How much income can my capital support, and what happens to its purchasing power?
1. Retirement assumptions
Guide: why inflation matters in retirement
Inflation affects both future spending needs and the purchasing power of capital. Preserving RM1 million nominally is not the same as preserving what RM1 million can buy today.
Example: at 3% inflation, RM1 million would need to grow to about RM1.81 million after 20 years to have roughly the same purchasing power.

3. Choose your retirement objective
Guide: understanding the three retirement profiles
Maximum Distribution finds the highest starting monthly income that aims to finish with at least the original capital in nominal ringgit. Preserve Real Capital aims to finish with enough nominal capital to preserve the starting capital's purchasing power after inflation. Balanced starts with a conservative 2.4% annual distribution rate and lets the portfolio continue working.
Same RM1 million, different objectives: Maximum Distribution normally allows more income than Preserve Real Capital because the latter requires a larger inflation-adjusted ending balance. Balanced is not a maximum-income solver; it deliberately starts conservatively.
